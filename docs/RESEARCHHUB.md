## What is a knot, and what is runaway release? A call for experiments

Bodyworkers, people who foam-roll and contemplative practitioners all describe knots: discrete places in the body that
hold, sometimes for years, and let go within seconds, under a hand, with the breath or to attention, often with a tingle
over the spot. They are among the commonest things a hand finds in a body and among the least explained. As far as is
known, nothing has been recorded at the moment one lets go.

A position paper, *The perforator hypothesis: knots as small arteries held shut where they pierce the fascia* (Zenodo,
https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23901648), argues from established physiology that a knot is a cutaneous perforator, a
small artery held shut by its own wall where it pierces the fascia. It sets out six alternatives in their strongest forms
(smooth muscle in the latch state, a myofascial trigger point, densified hyaluronan, a sensitised nerve, motor units that
latch on, and perception) and the measurements that would refute it. The project's site sets out all seven, each at its
strongest: https://ejhong.github.io/knots/hypotheses/ (code and data: https://github.com/ejhong/knots).

The paper was written by AI under its author's direction and has not been peer-reviewed, and its author will not take it
to a journal. If, having weighed the alternatives, you think the perforator hypothesis is the best fit, take it up: test
it, rework it, publish it (the paper is CC BY 4.0). A letter can propose that write-up, and it can be funded. If you think
a rival theory fits better, the models, data and code are open to you too.

**Runaway release.** Some who work forcefully and for long at clearing knots, in contemplative practice or in somatic
work, describe a rarer process. Past some point, releases come in chains and the process can no longer be set down: it
feels unfinished, and stopping is harder to bear than going on. Then come phases felt in the fascia, over days to weeks
and recurring over months or years: the layers peel apart along lines, places are left hollow, and a slow re-stitching in
the end brings back a sense of wholeness. The reports are many but informal, and no study of the process as such is known
(the position paper, Section 5). It is arduous and can be dangerous, above all for someone facing it alone: difficulties
of the wider kind it belongs to have lasted years, and in a study that recruited Western Buddhist meditators who could
describe difficult experiences, about one in six reported suicidality (Lindahl et al. 2017).

So this call has two prongs: **what a knot is**, and **what runaway release is**. Ten experiments that might help with
one or the other follow, numbered for reference and in no order of importance; they are also on the site:
https://ejhong.github.io/knots/research/#experiments.

Feeling a knot may itself be a skill, and a knot that its owner feels may or may not be one that others can feel.
Informal reports suggest that some who foam-roll can isolate a single knot, felt as a distinct bump, and release it
readily, perhaps through the breath, while others never feel single knots at all, only broad, dull masses. If so,
studies that locate or release knots cannot assume that anyone can find one, in themselves or in others, which is why
the first experiment asks who can.

**You are the experts.** These experiments are ideas from a non-expert working with AI, meant to show the kind of thing
that might help. You know the methods, the pitfalls and what is feasible: judge them, rework them, combine them, or
propose something better. The experiment you think would actually decide the question matters more than any listed here.

### The experiments

**1. Who can feel a knot: its owner, others, or both** (knots)
Two questions, which may have different answers: can people feel their own knots, and can others feel them too? A knot
might be felt only by its owner, or by a hand from outside as well. Many who foam-roll or work on their own knots might
be surveyed and interviewed: whether they can isolate a single knot, felt as a distinct bump, or feel only broad, dull
masses; whether they can release them; how they learned; and what part the breath plays. Bodyworkers might be asked the
same about the knots they feel in others. Then, in the same people, each might mark the knots they feel, blind to the
other marks: the person their own, twice, an hour apart, and two examiners by touch. Which knots does each find, and do
they agree?
*What it might show.* Whether feeling knots is itself a skill, perhaps tied to the breath as releasing them seems to be,
held by some and not by others; and whether the knots a person feels in themselves can be felt by others too, or only
from inside. If so, studies that locate or release knots would need to recruit those who have the skill they rely on.
*Likely needs:* a survey and interview researcher · many people who foam-roll, and bodyworkers · simple blind tests

**2. Catch a knot as it lets go** (knots)
People who can release knots at will (bodyworkers, practitioners skilled with a foam roller, a ball or the breath) might
release them in a laboratory while instruments watch the tissue: skin flow by laser speckle or a thermal camera,
ultrasound with colour Doppler and the layers' sliding, surface EMG resolved to single motor units. A button could mark
each felt release, with a spot pressed alike, without a knot, as the sham.
*What it might show.* Each of the seven proposed mechanisms would, on its own account, leave a different trace at
release, from a flood of flow to the skin to nothing in the tissue at all; a careful recording might begin to sort
releases by kind.
*Likely needs:* a laboratory with ultrasound and EMG or skin-flow imaging · a few skilled practitioners (experiment 1
might find them) · ethics review

**3. Characterise runaway release** (runaway release)
The case histories that fit its profile could be gathered, from published case reports, the record of meditation-related
difficulty, teachers, clinicians and support services, and people who have been through it, or are in it now,
interviewed with one set of questions: how it began and ran, its stages and regions, how long each took, what helped and
what made it worse.
*What it might show.* No study of it as such is known. A careful description might show whether it is one thing or
several, how common it is, who is at risk and what helps, and might find the participants for experiment 4.
*Likely needs:* a qualitative or clinical researcher, best with experience of meditation-related difficulty (Lindahl et
al. 2017; Cooper et al. 2021) · ethics review · support at hand for participants

**4. Examine someone in runaway release** (runaway release)
People already in runaway release might be examined while it runs and again as it settles: ultrasound of the fascial
layers and their glide where change is felt and at matched places, colour Doppler of the perforators, skin-flow imaging,
and the blood tests a physician would order. No one would be asked to begin or seek it, and their care would come first.
*What it might show.* If the tissue is changing, the glide might differ at the reported places and settle on the clock
the reports give; if only perception is changing, the tissue would likely look as it did.
*Likely needs:* a clinician-researcher with imaging who knows meditation-related difficulty · a route to participants ·
a plan for referral · ethics review

**5. Where knots sit** (knots)
Knots might be marked over a region of the body in two ways, or both (experiment 1): by two examiners who can feel knots
in others, blind to each other, or by people who can isolate their own. A third person, blind to the marks, might map
the perforators with a pencil Doppler, or with a thermal camera as the skin rewarms after a mild cold (Weum et al.
2016). The marks could then be set against the vessels at several distances, and against random and shifted maps.
*What it might show.* Whether knots sit on perforators (as the perforator and nerve views expect), where a muscle's
nerve enters (trigger points), or wherever stress is held (the other four); and, marked both ways, whether people's own
marks and an examiner's agree. Perhaps the cheapest test of the perforator view: an afternoon's work.
*Likely needs:* examiners who can feel knots in others, or people who can isolate their own · a pencil Doppler or
thermal camera · a statistician

**6. Whether a knot is a stable thing** (knots)
The same people's knots might be mapped again over weeks and months, against their own map of perforators: by two
examiners who can feel knots in others, blind to each other and to earlier maps, or by the people themselves, if they
can isolate their own (experiment 1), blind to their earlier marks.
*What it might show.* Whether the same knots are found again at all, which may depend on the skill of whoever marks
them, and whether a person's knots return to fixed places (as the perforator and nerve views expect) or arise anywhere
and drift. Much else may rest on the answer.
*Likely needs:* examiners, or people who can isolate their own knots · a few people, each seen several times · a Doppler
or thermal map

**7. What is already recorded** (knots)
Laboratories that study trigger points hold ultrasound, colour Doppler and elastography of tender spots, recorded for
other questions (Sikdar et al. 2009). Read again with the perforators in mind, these might say whether one runs beneath
the spot, in which layer any bump lies, and whether the layers glide over it.
*What it might show.* A first look, for the cost of the analysis: a bump in muscle would point toward a trigger point or
the motor switch, a perforator beneath toward perforators or nerves, and layers that do not glide toward densification.
*Likely needs:* access to existing recordings · someone who reads musculoskeletal ultrasound

**8. A small artery held shut for hours** (knots)
In a pressure myograph, a small artery, human if possible, might be shut with an agonist for five minutes or for four
hours, and the agonist then lowered to a resting level.
*What it might show.* Whether a vessel can remember. If knots are vessels that set, the first might reopen and the
second stay shut, opening past its resting diameter when finally released; if the second reopens as fully as the first,
that would count against vessels setting.
*Likely needs:* a laboratory with a pressure myograph · small arteries · a day's work

**9. The breath and the skin of the trunk** (knots)
The breath's effects on skin flow, such as the brief narrowing of skin vessels with a deep breath, are measured mostly
in the fingers. They could be measured over the back and neck, where knots are reported, with laser speckle: how far the
vessels there narrow, and how fast they relax once the sympathetic drive falls.
*What it might show.* Whether a vessel over the trunk could let go within one breath or would need many: timing that the
vascular accounts, the perforators and the latch, depend on.
*Likely needs:* laser speckle or laser Doppler · a physiology laboratory

**10. Acupoints on perforators** (knots)
Perforators might be mapped by thermal camera as the skin rewarms after a mild cold, and checked by Doppler, while
practitioners mark the classical points by the WHO's proportional rules, out of the reader's sight. Or, from records:
the channels could be laid over the CT angiograms taken before abdominal flap surgery, which record perforators by the
thousand.
*What it might show.* Whether the points sit on perforators more than chance would put them. On the five lines where
both are measured they sit only a little nearer than random points: not shown, not excluded.
*Likely needs:* a thermal camera and Doppler · practitioners and a reader, blind to each other · a statistician

### What to send: a letter of intent

A page or less, which shouldn't take long to write:

- the question, and what the result might show;
- what you would measure, in whom, and with what;
- who would do it, and their relevant experience;
- a rough timeline and a rough total cost.

One experiment, or part of one, is enough. Letters are weighed by what they might settle for what they would cost, so
small, decisive experiments are as welcome as large ones. [Letters under $X are especially welcome.] [Letters by DATE /
open until filled.]

### What happens next

Every letter will be read, and promising ones followed up with questions or an invitation to a full proposal[, with the
time a full proposal takes paid for]. Funded work shares its data and code, and keeps a simple public log of updates:
frequent one-liners are ideal ("first three participants recorded", "switched from thermal imaging to laser speckle").

### Care

Any study with people needs ethics review, ordinary breathing, comfortable pressure and a plan for referral; nothing here
is a practice or a protocol. No one should be asked to begin or seek runaway release: studies of it observe people
already in it, put their care first, and need clinicians who know meditation-related difficulty. If you are struggling
with it yourself, Cheetah House (https://www.cheetahhouse.org) supports meditators in distress, and anything physical
calls for a physician. If you are thinking about suicide, reach out now: in the US, call or text 988; elsewhere, find a
line at https://findahelpline.com.
